2.3-4

(1): Let the free charge density on the plates be $\sigma_{e0}$.

the displacement field is uniform across both dielectrics:  

$$D = \sigma_{e0}.\qquad E_1 = \frac{D}{\varepsilon_1} = \frac{\sigma_{e0}}{\varepsilon_1}, \quad E_2 = \frac{\sigma_{e0}}{\varepsilon_2}.$$


$$U = E_1 d_1 + E_2 d_2 = \sigma_{e0} \left( \frac{d_1}{\varepsilon_1} + \frac{d_2}{\varepsilon_2} \right).$$

The total charge is $Q = \sigma_{e0} S$, so:  

$$C = \frac{Q}{U} = \frac{\sigma_{e0} S}{\sigma_{e0} \left( \frac{d_1}{\varepsilon_1} + \frac{d_2}{\varepsilon_2} \right)}= \frac{S}{\frac{d_1}{\varepsilon_1} + \frac{d_2}{\varepsilon_2}}.$$

$$\boxed{C = \frac{S}{\frac{d_1}{\varepsilon_1} + \frac{d_2}{\varepsilon_2}}}$$



(2): Polarization vectors:  

$$
P_1 = D - \varepsilon_0 E_1 = \sigma_{e0} \left( 1 - \frac{\varepsilon_0}{\varepsilon_1} \right),
$$
$$
P_2 = \sigma_{e0} \left( 1 - \frac{\varepsilon_0}{\varepsilon_2} \right).
$$

$$\sigma_{p1} = P_1\quad  \sigma_{p2} = -P_2\qquad \sigma_e' = \sigma_{p1} + \sigma_{p2} = P_1 - P_2.$$



$$\sigma_e' = \sigma_{e0} \left[ \left(1 - \frac{\varepsilon_0}{\varepsilon_1}\right) - \left(1 - \frac{\varepsilon_0}{\varepsilon_2}\right) \right]
= \sigma_{e0} \varepsilon_0 \left( \frac{1}{\varepsilon_2} - \frac{1}{\varepsilon_1} \right).$$

$$
\boxed{\sigma_e' = \sigma_{e0} \varepsilon_0 \left( \frac{1}{\varepsilon_2} - \frac{1}{\varepsilon_1} \right)}
$$



(3): From the result in part (1):  

$$
\boxed{U = \sigma_{e0} \left( \frac{d_1}{\varepsilon_1} + \frac{d_2}{\varepsilon_2} \right)}
$$



 (4): Electric displacement $D$ in both dielectrics  

From Gauss’s law, $D$ is uniform across the dielectrics:  

$$
\boxed{D_1 = D_2 = \sigma_{e0}}
$$

2.3-13


(1) Potential difference $ U $ between the plates

The capacitor is divided into two parallel regions:  

dielectric area: area $ S/2 $, dielectric thickness $ t $, air thickness $ d-t $.  

  Capacitance:
  $$  C_A = \frac{S/2}{\dfrac{d-t}{\varepsilon_0} + \dfrac{t}{\varepsilon}}.$$

air area: area $ S/2 $, separation $ d $.  

  Capacitance:
  $$C_B = \frac{\varepsilon_0 S}{2d}.$$

Total capacitance (parallel):
$$C = C_A + C_B = \frac{S}{2} \left[ \frac{1}{\dfrac{d-t}{\varepsilon_0} + \dfrac{t}{\varepsilon}} + \frac{\varepsilon_0}{d} \right].$$

Potential difference:
$$\boxed{U = \frac{Q}{C} = \frac{Q}{\dfrac{S}{2} \left[ \dfrac{1}{\dfrac{d-t}{\varepsilon_0} + \dfrac{t}{\varepsilon}} + \dfrac{\varepsilon_0}{d} \right]}}$$



(2) Capacitance $ C $

$$\boxed{C = \frac{S}{2} \left[ \frac{1}{\dfrac{d-t}{\varepsilon_0} + \dfrac{t}{\varepsilon}} + \frac{\varepsilon_0}{d} \right]}$$



(3) Polarization charge surface density $ \sigma_e' $ on the dielectric

dielectric area:
$$D = \frac{Q_A}{S/2} = \frac{C_A U}{S/2}.$$
Polarization:
$$P = D \left( 1 - \frac{\varepsilon_0}{\varepsilon} \right).$$

$$\sigma_e' = P = D \left( 1 - \frac{\varepsilon_0}{\varepsilon} \right).\qquad D = \dfrac{Q}{S} \cdot \dfrac{2C_A}{C_A + C_B} $$

$$\boxed{\sigma_e' = \frac{Q}{S} \cdot \frac{2C_A}{C_A + C_B} \left( 1 - \frac{\varepsilon_0}{\varepsilon} \right)}$$

2.3-19



(1) Electric field $ E $ and displacement $ D $ 

Gauss’s law:
$$D = \frac{Q}{4\pi r^2} \quad (r > R).$$

$$ R < r < a :E = \frac{D}{\varepsilon_0} = \frac{Q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 r^2}.$$

$$ a < r < b :E = \frac{D}{\varepsilon} = \frac{Q}{4\pi\varepsilon r^2}.$$

$$ r > b :E = \frac{Q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 r^2}.$$

$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
&R<r<a:\quad D = \frac{Q}{4\pi r^2},\; E = \frac{Q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 r^2},\\
&a<r<b:\quad D = \frac{Q}{4\pi r^2},\; E = \frac{Q}{4\pi\varepsilon r^2},\\
&r>b:\quad D = \frac{Q}{4\pi r^2},\; E = \frac{Q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 r^2}.
\end{aligned}}
$$



(2) Polarization $ P $ and surface polarization charge density $ \sigma_e' $

$$P = D - \varepsilon_0 E = \frac{Q}{4\pi r^2} \left( 1 - \frac{\varepsilon_0}{\varepsilon} \right).$$

Surface polarization charge density ($ \sigma_p' = \mathbf{P} \cdot \mathbf{n}_{\text{out}} $):

$$\sigma_e'(a) = -P(a) = -\frac{Q}{4\pi a^2} \left( 1 - \frac{\varepsilon_0}{\varepsilon} \right).$$

$$\sigma_e'(b) = P(b) = \frac{Q}{4\pi b^2} \left( 1 - \frac{\varepsilon_0}{\varepsilon} \right).$$
$$
\boxed{P = \frac{Q}{4\pi r^2} \left( 1 - \frac{\varepsilon_0}{\varepsilon} \right),\quad
\sigma_e'(a) = -\frac{Q}{4\pi a^2} \left( 1 - \frac{\varepsilon_0}{\varepsilon} \right),\quad
\sigma_e'(b) = \frac{Q}{4\pi b^2} \left( 1 - \frac{\varepsilon_0}{\varepsilon} \right)}
$$



(3) Volume polarization charge density $ \rho_e' $ inside the dielectric

$$\rho_e' = -\nabla \cdot \mathbf{P}.$$

Since $ P(r) \propto 1/r^2 $, we have $ \nabla \cdot \mathbf{P} = 0 $, hence:

$$\boxed{\rho_e' = 0}$$

2.3-24

By Gauss’s law:

$$D(\rho) \cdot 2\pi\rho l = Q \quad \Rightarrow \quad D(\rho) = \frac{Q}{2\pi\rho l}.$$

Electric field:

$$E_1(\rho) = \frac{D}{\varepsilon_1} = \frac{Q}{2\pi\varepsilon_1 l \rho} \quad (a < \rho < r),$$

$$E_2(\rho) = \frac{Q}{2\pi\varepsilon_2 l \rho} \quad (r < \rho < b).$$

Voltage:

$$U = \int_a^r E_1 \, d\rho + \int_r^b E_2 \, d\rho= \frac{Q}{2\pi l} \left[ \frac{1}{\varepsilon_1} \ln\frac{r}{a} + \frac{1}{\varepsilon_2} \ln\frac{b}{r} \right].$$

Capacitance:

$$C = \frac{Q}{U} = \frac{2\pi l}{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_1} \ln\frac{r}{a} + \frac{1}{\varepsilon_2} \ln\frac{b}{r}}.$$

$$\boxed{C = \frac{2\pi l}{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_1} \ln\frac{r}{a} + \frac{1}{\varepsilon_2} \ln\frac{b}{r}}}$$

2.3-35


By Gauss’s law:$D(\rho) = \frac{\lambda}{2\pi\rho}$

$$E_1(\rho) = \frac{D}{\varepsilon_1} = \frac{\lambda}{2\pi\varepsilon_1 \rho} \quad (R_1 < \rho < r)\qquad E_2(\rho) = \frac{D}{\varepsilon_2} = \frac{\lambda}{\pi\varepsilon_1 \rho} \quad (r < \rho < R_2)$$
$$\rho = R_1^+ ,\quad E_{1,\max} = \frac{\lambda}{2\pi\varepsilon_1 R_1}\qquad \qquad \quad\rho = r^+ ,\quad E_{2,\max} = \frac{\lambda}{\pi\varepsilon_1 r}\qquad\qquad$$

$ E_{2,\max} = 2 \cdot \frac{R_1}{r} \cdot E_{1,\max} > E_{1,\max} ,\qquad$the outer dielectric breaks down first.



$$\lambda = \pi\varepsilon_1 r E_m ,\qquad E_1(\rho) = \frac{r E_m}{2\rho}, \quad E_2(\rho) = \frac{r E_m}{\rho}\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad$$


$$U_m = \int_{R_1}^{r} \frac{r E_m}{2\rho} \, d\rho + \int_{r}^{R_2} \frac{r E_m}{\rho} \, d\rho= \frac{r E_m}{2} \ln\frac{r}{R_1} + r E_m \ln\frac{R_2}{r}.$$

$$U_m = \frac{r E_m}{2} \left[ \ln\frac{r}{R_1} + 2\ln\frac{R_2}{r} \right]= \frac{r E_m}{2} \ln\frac{R_2^2}{r R_1}.$$

2.3-39

Before 

$$C_0 = \frac{\varepsilon_0 S}{d}, \quad Q_0 = C_0 U, \quad W_0 = \frac{1}{2} C_0 U^2$$

After 

$$C = \frac{\varepsilon S}{d}, \quad Q = C U, \quad W = \frac{1}{2} C U^2$$



(1) Change in electrostatic energy
$$\Delta W_e = W - W_0 = \frac{S U^2}{2d} (\varepsilon - \varepsilon_0).$$
$$\boxed{\Delta W_e = \frac{S U^2}{2d} (\varepsilon - \varepsilon_0)}$$



(2) Work done by the electric field on the battery  
Charge increment:
$$\Delta Q = Q - Q_0 = \frac{S U}{d} (\varepsilon - \varepsilon_0)$$

Work done by the battery (energy supplied by battery):
$$W_{\text{source}} = U \cdot \Delta Q = \frac{S U^2}{d} (\varepsilon - \varepsilon_0)$$
$$\boxed{W_{\text{source}} = \frac{S U^2}{d} (\varepsilon - \varepsilon_0)}$$



(3) Work done by the electric field on the dielectric slab  
Energy conservation:
$$W_{\text{source}} = \Delta W_e + W_{\text{diel}}$$

$$W_{\text{diel}} = W_{\text{source}} - \Delta W_e= \frac{S U^2}{2d} (\varepsilon - \varepsilon_0)$$

$$\boxed{W_{\text{diel}} = \frac{S U^2}{2d} (\varepsilon - \varepsilon_0)}$$
